# Landing simulation mechanics

## Engine state

The landing simulator carries left and right engine state through each physics step. It passes the two actual thrust values to the existing flight integrator. Engine RPM is normalized to the game's scale. The engine state codes are Off (0), Starting (1), Running (2), and Dead (3).

These values come from the current Demo's `GameAssembly.dll`. The addresses are RVAs in that build; after a game update, look the functions up by name.

| Behavior | Native evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Spool and actual thrust | `EngineUnit.ServerTick` at `0x6B3890` moves a running engine's spool toward bounded throttle at 0.55/s up and 0.8/s down. Spool decays at 1.0/s when off or dead. Running thrust uses `spool × (0.45 + 0.55 × health)` before fault and ice multipliers. Starting, off, and dead engines produce zero thrust. `PlaneFlight.EngineThrust` at `0xAFBAA0` passes engine telemetry into the flight simulation. |
| RPM and restart | The clear startup path waits 2.6 s before entering Running. The starter RPM target rises from 0.06 to 0.264 and the running target follows spool and thrust derating. Off and dead engines can windmill when airborne. `StartBlocker` at `0x6B5830` also checks conditions such as fire, an active swap, battery, pump bus, and fuel feed. |
| Fire | `ServerIgnite` at `0x6B0200` sets the fire flag and raises intensity to at least 0.35. A running engine under fire keeps 70% of the otherwise available thrust. `ServerTick` raises intensity by 0.035/s toward 1, drains health at `0.01 × (1 + intensity)` per second, and destroys the engine once health reaches zero. `ServerDouseFire` at `0x6AED40` subtracts the extinguishing amount from intensity. |
| Wrench repair and replacement | `ServerWrenchTick` at `0x6B5180` resets its work timer following a gap longer than 2 s. It resolves work at 4 s and one health repair branch adds 0.30, capped at 1. `ServerBeginSwap` at `0x6AD960` checks for an open or missing cowl. `ServerSwapWork` at `0x6B33D0` divides accumulated work by 10, and `ServerFinishSwap` at `0x6AEF10` leaves the replacement Off with health 1. |
| Observed repair and network state | `RepairProgress` at `0x6B8C00` presents `swapP` during a swap and `repair` otherwise. The native SyncVar writer and reader at `0x6AD5D0` and `0x6A9AD0` carry these values and RPM, thrust, health, and fire intensity. On a host, the public RPM, thrust, health, and fire-intensity getters use the local values. On a client they use the synchronized values. `OnFire` reads the separate fault bit `0x20`. |

The simulation starts with two running engines at the initial throttle equilibrium. Scenarios author the time and side of failure, extinguishing, service access, work, shutdown, and restart. A serviceable repair uses normalized work, holds progress during a scripted pause, and adds 0.30 health on completion. A dead engine requires a completed replacement and a later start. The swap input represents an authorized request after any interaction hold, and work represents seconds contributed while replacing the engine. The scenario handles battery, fuel, pumps, tool cadence, and access eligibility at the input boundary. The simulator advances these states at physics-step cadence. The game updates its engines from Unity `Update`.

## Weather

The optional [weather profiles](../tools/mayday_landing/weather.py) cover clear conditions, rain, fog, wind gusts, fog with wind gusts, and an icing incident. A seed controls profile selection and position relative to the runway. These are approach scenarios with chosen event weights and start conditions. The native world schedules fronts over a whole route. `DynAt` draws from the live world records or the local cell list, and `SampleAt` blends these spatial cells with front and biome inputs. Native sampling and cell access

| Mechanism | Native evidence and profile behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Visibility and fog | `FogVisibility` at `0xCC3650` applies `1500 − 1180 × smoothstep(0.2, 1, severity)` and rounds to 10 m. Legs up to 3 have a 1,200 m floor and legs 4–5 have a 900 m floor. Later legs can reach 320 m. `FogRadius` and `FogTop` give `4500 + 1500 × severity` and `70 + 90 × severity` meters. `BankFog` fades across the outer 20% of the bank and from 40 m below to 30 m above its top. `FogFrame` starts at 8,000 m and uses the visibility of active banks. The profile uses one fully established bank at the runway. Fog-only leaves thrust, wind, turbulence, and surface friction unchanged. |
| Rain and turbulence | `AccumulateCell` at `0xCBC550` uses the rain envelope `(0.55 + 0.45 × severity) × radial fade`, with the fade beginning at 30% radius. `ApplyFront` at `0xCBCD20` applies separate rain-front and wind-front turbulence floors. The profile places one seeded rain cell along the approach and uses a reproducible sinusoid for front modulation. It retains the 8,000 m clear visibility field outside fog and the integrator's surface-based runway friction. |
| Wind and gusts | `ServerStartCrosswind` at `0xB10040` selects a lateral wind of `5.5 + 3 × difficulty` m/s. `UpdateCrosswind` ramps the event over 6 s and modulates its magnitude by up to 18%. `FrontGusts` emits wind-front gusts once blend reaches 0.5 and samples the next interval from 18–32 s divided by `0.7 + 0.6 × severity`. The profile treats the front as established, uses one seeded interval per approach, and emits the 0.3 s downward-heave pulse from `ApplyTurbulence`. Each front gust is `Hazards.Raise(5)`. `PlaneFlight.OnHazard` (`0xB06850`) then sets `pendingGust` and draws `gustDir = ±1` with even odds. On the next airborne tick, `ApplyTurbulence` adds `12 × gustDir`° to bank (clamped to ±75°) and `2 × gustDir`° to heading, in addition to the 14 m/s² heave for 0.3 s. The twin models this kick only with `--gust-kick` (`Scenario.gust_kick`); a seeded hash per gust stands in for the native draw. The existing reference approach still supplies its seeded continuous disturbance when this profile is disabled. |
| Icing | `SampleAt` gates icing on moisture above 0.2 and ramps it over 600 m above the freezing level. `HullSystem.ServerTick` at `0x716160` accumulates airframe ice at `0.012 × icing` per second while exposed, sheds at 0.004/s outside exposure, and sheds at 0.03/s with powered deice. Engine thrust scales by `1 − 0.35 × clamp(airframe ice, 0, 1)`. The icing scenario injects a timed incident on final and starts with some previous accumulation. Its elapsed-time projection assumes airborne exposure for the incident duration, then passive thaw. The separate step helper accepts actual airborne and deice flags. |

The optional profiles provide scalar weather observations and physical inputs at the aircraft. Fog rendering and cockpit wetness remain in the game. Native fog symbols · Front gust and cell sampling · Native gust application · Crosswind update · Icing branch

## Verification

The mechanics tests cover left/right spool and windmilling, asymmetric thrust during fire, extinguishing, paused repair and restart, the dead-engine replacement path, weather inputs, and a JAX scan over engine state.

The existing JAX integrator replay passed 27,376 ticks from a host recording with zero flag mismatches. Its maximum position error was 0.000488281 m and maximum speed error was 0.00000762939 m/s. The CSV records delivered thrust for both engines after upstream engine logic. Both recorded outputs vary between 0 and 1 together, and the capture has no engine health, RPM, fire, or repair fields. The native-integrator check supports using actual thrust at the physics boundary. Validation of upstream fault and service timing still requires recordings of those events and their engine telemetry.

**Landing gear jam (native).** `ServerJamGear` (`0xB0DCC0`) records the jam stop as the current extension, or 0.7 when the gear was above 0.9. Each crank use (`ServerCrankGear`, `0xB0C480`) adds 0.125 to the stop, up to 1.0. `FixedUpdate` clears the jam once the stop reaches 1.0 and the leg reaches 0.999. A hammer repair (`ServerHammerGear`) also clears it. The twin models the crank with `--gear-crank`.

**Touchdown outcome (native).** `PlaneFlight.OnTouchdown` (`0xB07A30`) crashes the aircraft only when sink exceeds 8 m/s, bank exceeds 30°, the surface is invalid, or a field landing fails. Bank between `WingStrikeRoll` (9.06° in the recorded tuning) and 30° raises damage hazard 6 (`HazardType.Collision`, wing strike). Touching down with the gear unlocked, or at a sink of 2.5 m/s or more, raises hazard 7 (`HazardType.HardLanding`). This was confirmed in game: a manual touchdown at about 3.7 m/s raised HardLanding. Drift above 10 raises a scaled hazard 6. The twin's `CRASH` termination (gear ≤ 0.9 or bank > `wing_strike_roll`) is therefore stricter than the game and should be read as a landing-quality failure. `taxonomy.native_crash` counts the native rule separately.

**Gust kick, confirmed in game (2026-11-22, host recording).** Three native `SevereGust` events changed bank within one frame by +12.06°, −12.22° and +12.05°, always in the sign of `gustDir`, and changed heading by ±2.0°. `gustT` then counted down from 0.283 s. The twin's `--gust-kick` model therefore matches the game.

**Rollout devices (native).** `CmdPilotAction(4)` maps to control 0xD (`ServerSetReverse`, which toggles). The server denies reverse while airborne. In the twin, reverse turns 60% of throttle thrust backwards. `CmdPilotAction(2)` maps to control 4 (`ServerSetHandbrake`, also a toggle). A handbrake set at 12 m/s or faster on the ground raises a passenger panic, tyre smoke effects and a counter at PlaneFlight+0x378; above 3 m/s the twin marks the aircraft as skidding. The parking-brake deceleration is 5.0 m/s², against 3.6 for the wheel brakes.
